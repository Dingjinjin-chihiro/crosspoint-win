A SHORT WALK

It began, as these things do, with a door that would not close.

The street was empty in the way that streets are empty at six in the morning,
when the light is grey and everything looks slightly borrowed. She walked to
the corner, turned left for no reason, and kept going until the houses gave up
and the fields began.

There is a kind of silence that only exists outside, away from walls. It is not
the absence of sound but the presence of distance. You can hear a dog bark a
kilometre away; you can hear a train breathing behind a hill.

By the time she came back, the door had closed itself. She never found out how.

THE END
